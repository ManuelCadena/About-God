**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

**Title:**

**The Evidence for God: A Probabilistic Model of Divine Existence and the Role of Moral Evil**

**Objective**

This study develops a rigorous, interdisciplinary **logistic regression model** to estimate the **probability that God exists**, based on **eight evidential variables** drawn from cosmology, philosophy, neuroscience, and theology. The model combines these factors using **Bayesian reasoning** and **Monte Carlo simulations**, enabling a data-informed, epistemically transparent approach to one of the most foundational questions in human inquiry.

**Model Structure**

Each variable represents a major class of arguments for or against God's existence:

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Code**   **Variable**               **Influence (Expected)**
  ---------- -------------------------- -------------------------------------------
  FT         Cosmological Fine-Tuning   Strongly pro-theism

  MO         Moral Objectivity          Moderately pro-theism

  CG         Consciousness Gap          Moderately pro-theism

  RE         Religious Experience       Slightly pro-theism

  EV         Moral Evil                 **Explored as both neutral and negative**

  BC         Biological Complexity      Moderately pro-theism

  QM         Quantum Indicators         Slightly pro-theism

  MN         Metaphysical Necessity     Strongly pro-theism
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Key Methodology**

- A prior of **P(God) = 50%** was assumed (epistemic neutrality)

- Each variable's coefficient β was assigned based on philosophical and empirical literature

- **100,000 Monte Carlo simulations** were run to capture uncertainty in all weights

- A full **decision tree** visualizes how combinations of evidence affect outcomes

- **Appendix C** explores the impact of treating **moral evil as negative evidence** (β_EV \< 0)

**Results (Main Model, EV Neutralized)**

- **Median P(God):** \~98.6%

- **Mean P(God):** \~94.6%

- **P(God) \> 90%:** in \~85% of simulations

- **P(God) \< 50%:** in \<1.3% of simulations

→ The evidence strongly favors theism under modestly positive assumptions for fine-tuning, consciousness, and moral realism.

**Results (Appendix C -- EV Negative)**

- **Median P(God):** \~95--97%

- **Mean P(God):** \~90--92%

- **P(God) \< 50%:** rises to \~3--5%

→ Treating moral evil as evidentially negative reduces certainty but **does not overturn theism** under most plausible scenarios.

**Philosophical Highlights**

- The **cumulative case** for God remains strong even when moral evil is given evidential weight.

- The model supports a **balanced, non-dogmatic view**: the existence of evil *lowers* confidence in God, but fine-tuning, consciousness, and metaphysical grounding typically outweigh it.

- The framework allows readers to **adjust assumptions** and observe corresponding shifts in probability --- enabling honest exploration across belief spectra.

**Conclusion**

This paper demonstrates that belief in God can be rationally justified through a transparent, interdisciplinary, and quantitative model. While interpretations of key variables (especially moral evil) significantly affect outcomes, the **overall structure of reality**---from the laws of physics to human cognition---appears **more probable on theism than on atheism**.
