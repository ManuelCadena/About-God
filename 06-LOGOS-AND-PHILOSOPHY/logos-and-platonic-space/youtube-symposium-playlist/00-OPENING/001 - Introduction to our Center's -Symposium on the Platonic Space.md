## Transcripción

Welcome to the first symposium on the platonic space and its practical consequences in biology, physics, computer science and philosophy organized by myself, Michael Leven and Hanel Hazan. This symposium is brought to you by the Allen Discovery Center at Tufts University and the Institute for Computationally Designed Organisms at Tufts University and the University of Vermont. We want to thank our benefactors, the late Paul Allen for founding the Allen Discovery Center at Tufts and John Abley for supporting us at the ICTO. In this symposium, we're going to cover a number of topics including the nature of explanation in various fields, especially in mathematics, and what happens to explaining a phenomenon that crosses multiple fields. We will also address causation in various fields especially causation by non-physical or mathematical objects. Uh we will discuss examples of how the properties of mathematical objects determine outcomes in physics and biology. We will ask questions such as where do patterns of form and behavior come from in biology, physics and other sciences and can we do better than the idea of emergence for prediction and control of novel phenomena. And most of all we will discuss how to use these ideas to move empirical work forward in computer science, biology, engineering and many other disciplines.
