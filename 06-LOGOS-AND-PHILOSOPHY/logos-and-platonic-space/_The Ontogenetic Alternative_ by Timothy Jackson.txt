Complete Summary of Timothy Jackson's Ontogenetic Alternative Lecture 
Speaker Introduction and Background 
Timothy Jackson introduces himself as an evolutionary biologist, chemical ecologist, pharmacologist, and philosopher of science ‎‎ 00:00:30
. His research focuses on connecting molecular evolution to chemical ecology to understand the genesis of novel functions ‎‎ 00:00:40
. Jackson co-directs the Process Science Project with Matt Siegel, hosted by the Centre for Process Studies, which reframes the history of modern science and evolutionary thought while auditing contemporary research programs ‎‎ 00:01:29
. 
Core Thesis: The Ontogenetic Method 
Jackson presents the ontogenetic method as an alternative to both Platonism and traditional materialism for understanding morphogenesis and open-ended evolution ‎‎ 00:01:11
. The method draws heavily from Gilbert Simondon's work on individuation, treating ontogenesis (the genesis of being) as first philosophy ‎‎ 00:06:34
. 
Critique of Substantialism and Hylomorphism 
Substantialism Problems 
Jackson identifies substantialism as one problematic approach to understanding individuation, characterized by obsession with primary substances that everything else can be reduced to ‎‎ 00:07:04
. He quotes Simondon's definition: "The being as consisting in its unity, given to itself, founded on itself, not engendered and as resistant to what is not itself" ‎‎ 00:07:23
. 
Hylomorphism Analysis 
Hylomorphism, the doctrine that individuals result from encounters between form and matter, receives extensive critique ‎‎ 00:07:37
. Jackson argues that both substantialism and hylomorphism assume a principle of individuation prior to individuation itself, which is question-begging ‎‎ 00:08:16
. They attempt to understand individuation through pre-constituted individuals rather than examining the process of individuation directly ‎‎ 00:08:37
. 
Simondon's alternative approach represents individuation as "a splitting, a resolution, a non-symmetrical distribution occurring in a totality starting from a singularity" ‎‎ 00:09:30
. The individuated individual is incomplete, and individuation cannot be explained merely through form and matter analysis after the fact ‎‎ 00:10:09
. 
Mathematical Platonism and Its Limitations 
Historical Context 
Jackson examines various approaches to mathematical foundations, noting options beyond Platonism including constructivism, intuitionism, formalism, and logicism ‎‎ 00:11:12
. He references the contrast between Gödel (Platonist) and Hilbert (formalist) to illustrate these alternatives ‎‎ 00:11:23
. 
Critique of Mathematical Reductionism 
While appreciating mathematics, Jackson warns against ending up "in the maths department" when trying to solve problems in biology, physics, or philosophy ‎‎ 00:11:47
. He argues that purely mathematical approaches conceal as much as they reveal, advocating for transdisciplinary approaches ‎‎ 00:12:17
. 
Jackson presents Stephen Wolfram's observations about traditional mathematics avoiding complex patterns that appear uninteresting despite their natural importance ‎‎ 00:13:13
. He emphasizes that mathematics is "much more a product of its history than is usually realized" ‎‎ 00:14:10
. 
The Chora and Choric Materialism 
Platonic Receptacle 
Jackson recovers Plato's concept of the chora from the Timaeus as a "mass of plastic material" that serves as a "matrix or womb for everything" ‎‎ 00:21:14
. The chora must be "devoid of character" to receive every kind of character ‎‎ 00:21:25
. 
Choric Necessity 
The doctrine of choric necessity reveals that the world emerged from "a mixture and combination of necessity and intelligence" ‎‎ 00:21:55
. Necessity represents the chora itself, not intelligence, which belongs to the demiurge ‎‎ 00:22:14
. The chora exhibits endogenous forces creating non-equilibrium conditions that lead to constructive coarse-graining and incipient element formation ‎‎ 00:23:47
. 
Effective Theory and Its Constraints 
Definition and Function 
Jackson draws on David Krakauer's analysis of effective theories as discovering "large groupings (coarse-grainings) of fundamental or non-fundamental materials and properties that maintain coherence through time" ‎‎ 00:33:02
. These theories identify invariants for understanding variation while treating everything else as noise ‎‎ 00:33:28
. 
Limitations of Effective Theorization 
Effective theories define predetermined ontologies through closed spaces of possibility ‎‎ 00:35:00
. This approach struggles with ontogenesis, which appears open-ended ‎‎ 00:35:25
. Jackson traces this limitation to Laplace's deterministic vision of the universe as an automaton where "chance is merely ignorance" ‎‎ 00:36:38
. 
The Laplacean model requires external formal causes (entailing laws) and arbitrary initial conditions, representing a "total hegemonic formal cause" that eliminates degrees of freedom ‎‎ 00:38:20
. 
Information and Alterity 
Simondon on Information Amplification 
Jackson explains Simondon's 1962 analysis of information reception: "virtually any reality that does not fully possess the determination of the course of its becoming within itself is a potential receiver" ‎‎ 00:38:49
. Systems must contain internal tension capable of being selected or elicited by external alterity ‎‎ 00:39:12
. 
The Problem of Closure 
Any closed system modeling its own evolution must assume external parameters it cannot generate ‎‎ 00:42:11
. These parameters represent formal alterities that are "unpresstable, untraceable, and unmodelable from within" ‎‎ 00:42:11
. This demonstrates that such systems are not actually closed and that "the universe cannot be unitary" ‎‎ 00:42:11
. 
Darwin's Revolutionary Logic 
Variation Before Form 
Jackson identifies Darwin's major intervention as making "the overproduction of variation, difference, excess, the first principle" against special creation's hylomorphic thesis ‎‎ 00:50:48
. The Darwin-Peirce-Lamarck triad places variation first, followed by selection and inheritance ‎‎ 00:50:55
. 
Jerome Rosanvalon's "Darwin-Hooker principle" describes "a pure variability of the living, an inherent tendency to vary, which remains without determined or perhaps determinable cause" ‎‎ 00:51:22
. Natural selection intervenes only secondarily while tending to conserve and amplify this intrinsic variability ‎‎ 00:51:22
. 
Neo-Darwinism as Regression 
Jackson argues that Neo-Darwinism represents a hylomorphic regression rather than following Darwin's logic ‎‎ 00:52:30
. Bodies become matter constrained by genetic forms, returning to an artisanal model where genes inform matter ‎‎ 00:52:42
. This contradicts Darwin's anti-mechanistic approach where primary variation cannot be reduced to mechanical processes ‎‎ 00:52:56
. 
Physical versus Vital Individuation 
Simondon's Distinction 
Physical systems "individuate all at once" and "jump the gun" in their becoming ‎‎ 00:53:55
. Living systems "defer complete individuation indefinitely" through neoteny, maintaining juvenile characteristics and preserving their charge of pre-individual reality ‎‎ 00:54:22
. 
Organisms comprise "the coupling of individuated and pre-individual realities" rather than being merely what is already individuated ‎‎ 00:54:37
. They maintain internal problematics and are "not unitary" but "at odds with themselves" ‎‎ 00:54:55
. 
Individuation as Elicitation 
Relational Process 
Individuation occurs relationally when external alterity "selects, or refers to, or elicits, a subset of the organism subject's constitutive variation" ‎‎ 00:55:22
. This creates encounters between external and internal alterities in an "evental mode of elicitation" ‎‎ 00:55:32
. 
Elicitation differs from determination, being secondary to self-selection through "organizational canalization of constitutive variations" ‎‎ 00:55:57
. Living involves being "agent, milieu, and element of individuation" simultaneously ‎‎ 00:56:30
. 
The Ontogenetic Alternative Framework 
Core Principles 
The ontogenetic method studies "the being whose being is becoming" across all types of beings ‎‎ 00:57:25
. It avoids both substantialist and hylomorphic paths by not presupposing what it hopes to explain ‎‎ 00:58:05
. Starting with the indefinite rather than pre-formed entities, it embraces "variation first, autokinesis, spontaneity" ‎‎ 00:58:38
. 
Form as Emergent 
Form emerges as a consequence of individuation rather than preceding it ‎‎ 00:59:41
. Singularities are "evental" and "a praesenti" (present), counter-effectuating the virtual or potential ‎‎ 01:00:02
. The "adjacent possible halos the actual" as a penumbra of possibilities associated with actualized things ‎‎ 01:00:21
. 
Bodies are not completely constrained by form, which "arises via in-formative relations between active bodies" ‎‎ 01:01:17
. This represents "immanent genesis" where forms are "never absolutely finalized" even when appearing stable ‎‎ 01:01:26
. 
Challenging Physicalist Sacred Cows 
Questioning Fundamental Assumptions 
Jackson identifies "sacred cows of physicalist rationalism" including global unitarity, global principle of sufficient reason, and complete explanations ‎‎ 01:02:45
. He argues these may be impossible while maintaining that local versions remain valid ‎‎ 01:02:59
. 
The absence of a "perspective from which you could possibly understand or encompass the reason of all things" doesn't threaten science but acknowledges ontological limits ‎‎ 01:03:13
. These assumptions don't exist similarly in South and East Asian philosophical traditions, suggesting they may be artifacts of Western technical paradigms ‎‎ 01:04:14
. 
Life as Effective Theorization 
Organisms versus Machines 
Jackson proposes that "living systems are not machines; machines are modeled on living systems" ‎‎ 01:04:31
. Machines result from abstracting certain functions from organisms by organisms, making techne internal to the life world ‎‎ 01:04:48
. 
Organisms function as "effective theorizers, not effective theories" ‎‎ 01:05:06
. Since life is effective theorization, "there just can't be an effective theory of life" because you cannot encompass the process of creating theories within a theory itself ‎‎ 01:05:28
. 
Evental Elicitation and Haecceity 
Kairotic Encounters 
Jackson concludes with the concept of "evental elicitation" through kairotic (opportune moment) encounters ‎‎ 01:06:05
. Drawing on Deleuze and Guattari's interpretation of Simondon, he discusses haecceity as "a mode of individuation very different from that of a person, a subject, a thing, or substance" ‎‎ 01:06:22
. 
The term's alternative spelling "ecceity" (from "ecce," meaning "here is") represents "a fruitful error" suggesting individuation distinct from pre-constituted terms ‎‎ 01:06:51
. For Simondon, information's haecceity is "the here is, the hinc et nunc, the here and now" ‎‎ 01:07:13
. 
Perception and Pre-formed Images 
Using the example of finding cryptic snakes, Jackson illustrates how pre-formed mental representations can obstruct perceiving actual singularities ‎‎ 01:08:21
. This demonstrates why perception shouldn't be understood in hylomorphic terms, as prior constraints limit recognition of present realities. 
Conclusion 
Jackson concludes that "physis is apeiron, is boundless" ‎‎ 01:08:31
, returning to Anaximander's concept of the indefinite as fundamental reality. The ontogenetic alternative offers a framework for understanding morphogenesis and evolution that avoids the limitations of both mathematical Platonism and mechanistic materialism while preserving the creative, open-ended nature of natural processes.
