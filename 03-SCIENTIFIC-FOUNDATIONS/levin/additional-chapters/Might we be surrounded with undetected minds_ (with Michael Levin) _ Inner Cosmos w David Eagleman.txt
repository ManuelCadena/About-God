Summary 
This interview mainly discussed the concept of intelligence beyond traditional definitions, featuring neuroscientist David Eagleman interviewing biologist Michael Levin from Tufts University. Eagleman introduces the episode by questioning what intelligence is and suggesting it might exist in unexpected places beyond brains. Levin defines intelligence as "the ability to reach the same goal by different means" and argues that intelligence is "scale-free," existing at various levels from single cells to societies. The conversation explores how biological systems demonstrate problem-solving abilities when faced with obstacles or perturbations. Levin provides examples including embryonic development that adapts to novel circumstances, "Picasso tadpoles" with scrambled facial features that still develop into normal frogs, and xenobots/anthrobots (engineered living organisms made from frog and human cells respectively). They discuss how these examples challenge our understanding of intelligence as merely brain-based, suggesting instead that intelligence exists wherever systems navigate toward goals despite obstacles. The interview concludes with reflections on the ethical implications of recognizing diverse intelligences and how this perspective might influence our understanding of artificial intelligence, suggesting that we are surrounded by various forms of minds that we typically fail to recognize.
