Summary 
This interview mainly discussed complex topics at the intersection of developmental biology, evolutionary biology, cognitive science, and philosophy. Dr. Tevin Naidu moderated a conversation between Professor Terrence Deacon and Professor Michael Levin, two distinguished scientists with overlapping interests. The discussion began with both professors expressing mutual admiration for each other's work, particularly highlighting their shared interest in breaking down Cartesian dichotomies and exploring the deep commonalities underlying biological systems. They discussed philosopher Dan Dennett's influence on their thinking about the relationship between philosophy and science. The conversation then explored the origin of purpose in physical systems, with Deacon approaching this from a thermodynamic perspective and Levin discussing scaling and transformation models. They delved into concepts of regeneration and memory, particularly examining how planaria can regrow heads while retaining memories, with both professors offering complementary perspectives on compression/decompression processes in biological systems. The discussion concluded with reflections on teleology versus teleonomy, with Deacon arguing for systems that generate their own ends and Levin suggesting that even minimal systems can exhibit goal-directed behaviors not explicitly programmed into them.

Michael Levin added that he's interested in models of scaling and transformation, and the concept of "polycomputing" where nested observers within living structures continuously interpret each other. 
When a planaria regrows its head and retains memories, where do you think those memories are stored? And what does this tell us about the body as memory substrate? ‎‎ 00:40:10
Terrence Deacon discussed how memory assumes much of what they had already talked about, particularly the concept of compression. He explained that DNA is a compression, and what's important is the decompression process - not all information is stored in the DNA but rather in a highly compressed system paired with a decompression system. Michael Levin added that biology works with an unreliable medium where mutation is guaranteed, and suggested that living systems have problem solvers "all the way down" that aren't just mechanically doing what they do but have homeodynamic and other problem-solving capacities.

Terrence Deacon mentioned that some of Michael Levin's earlier work with Planaria troubled him initially, as it sounded like "pre-formationism" - the idea that there was an electrical map of how things were supposed to go. However, upon reading further, he realized Levin's work converged with his own thinking about decompression and reinterpretation logic. Deacon was particularly interested in Levin's later work showing that it's not a pre-formed map but a complicated interpretive process that can be biased in interesting ways.

Michael Levin mentioned that he didn't disagree with anything but recalled Deacon's discussion of teleonomy as a watered-down version of teleology. Levin explained that he takes teleology very seriously and uses teleonomy differently - not as a way to placate those who don't like actual teleology, but to remind us that what we observe is from the perspective of an observer.
